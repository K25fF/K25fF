#  Chicoutimi Nightfall
_In 1965, an overnight radio host in Chicoutimi receives a call from himself, and keeps him talking through eleven minutes of dead air._
{: .subtitle}

Michel Laurent – billed on air by his surname, as was the habit – held the
overnight shift at CJCI-FM from 1962 until 1991.

Transcribed 1966, from the station's logger tape. Translated from the French; the translator's notes are marked.

> ## \[BEGIN LOG]
>
> **03:14:** \[*crackle, as the record finishes*]
>
> **M.L.:** Can't go wrong with Louvain, hein? Now, what's n– \[*pause*] Oh.
> We have a caller. Can't get to sleep either? Good evening, caller, you're on the air. Who do I have?
>
> **CALLER:** La– \[*static*] –nt Michel. \[*?*]
> 
> **M.L.:** Ah, what a coincidence! We must be cousins. \[*laughs*]
> 
> **CALLER:** I've been trying to reach you.
> 
> **M.L.:** How flattering, to be in demand. Is something troubling you?
> 
> **CALLER:** Do \[*static*] ever feel like we're[^1] lost?
> 
> **M.L.:** Oh? Well, certainly, it was a tricky thing, when I first worked here, to figure out the drive, but–
> 
> **CALLER:** –that's not what you mean, is it?
> 
> **M.L.:** Ah, you're right. Sometimes, the world is big, ouais? You look around, and you've got no idea where you're going. But, you have to realise that no-one else has any idea where the– where they're going, don't they? I try to take comfort in that. I'm as lost as anyone else.
> 
> **CALLER:** I'm not sure I've thought that, but it makes sense.
> 
> **M.L.:** Hopefully that helps, then. Positive thinking, oui? You've got to think positive, at this time of night.
> 
> **CALLER:** I think we do.
> 
> **M.L.:** Oui, good to hear we agree.
> 
> **CALLER:** I had a dream, once. It's stupid, you'll think it's stupid.
> 
> **M.L.:** Oh, non, it's three in the morning, friend, nothing's stupid. It's just us and the bats.
> 
> **CALLER:** I was somewhere under. Below, and it was dark. It– no, it wasn't water. I couldn't get up to it, to the surface. There was light, above, coming down, coming down in patches, through holes.
> 
> **M.L.:** \[*laughs*] Just like the ceiling here.
> 
> **CALLER:** I suppose. I kept thinking they were mine.
> 
> **M.L.:** Mm?
> 
> **CALLER:** The holes. That they were my eyes. That I was looking up, at the inside of my own face.
>
> \[*silence, 4 s*]
>
> **M.L.:** Ouais. Ouais, I've had – everyone's had one like that.
> 
> **CALLER:** I knew we'd understand. \[*pause*] Could I request a song?
> 
> **M.L.:** Of course, of course, friend. I'll play it once we're done talking. Now, if I can just find... my pencil...
> 
> **CALLER:** On the left.
> 
> **M.L.:** Hm? Oh, merci!
> 
> **CALLER:** Bienvenue.
> 
> **M.L.:** So, what was your request?
> 
> **CALLER:** Louvain, please. Your choice.
> 
> **M.L.:** Hein, you've got taste. We just had him on. \[*pause*] You can't hear the pencil, but I'm making a note.
> 
> **CALLER:** Merci.
> 
> **M.L.:** A favourite of yours?
> 
> **CALLER:** I've heard his songs a lot. You can't escape them, but it's not unpleasant.
> 
> **M.L.:** \[*laughs*] Well, I host radio, so I can understand the feeling. I wouldn't want to play something too energetic at this time of night.
> 
> **CALLER:** Or fall asleep on the air.
> 
> **M.L.:** Oui, I have coffee for that. Oh, imagine the angry letters, if it was just hours of me snoring. Non, non, I get my sleep during the day like a good little owl. Anyway, what's left you awake at this hour?
> 
> **CALLER:** Sometimes, it's difficult to sleep.
> 
> **M.L.:** All too common.
> 
> **CALLER:** So, we go outside, to listen to the stars. To that new music[^2].
> 
> **M.L.:** Hein, usually when people say that, they're complaining of the din the kids are making. And out at night listening – you do DX[^3]?
> 
> **CALLER:** Yes, \[*static*] –are calling from \[*static*] darkness, and a long way out.
> 
> **M.L.:** Hear anything good lately? One of the guys at the club says he hears Lisbon, on good nights, but... I have my doubts.
> 
> **CALLER:** If you listen right, you can hear yourself.
> 
> **M.L.:** Oh yes. Spooky, isn't it[^4]? I always figured it was bouncing off something. Maybe it's the moon.
> 
> **CALLER:** Perhaps it's further.
> 
> **M.L.:** Perhaps so! Probably for the scientists to figure, hm? Myself, I'm usually catching Chicago or Boston, further on a clear night, when the lights aren't out.
> 
> **CALLER:** The nights can be noisy with light, with hum. With a twitch of a dial, one can listen in, catching songs from afar.
> 
> **M.L.:** Oh, oui, they complain at the club about the smelter over in Arvida – they say it buzzes on the airwaves.
> 
> **CALLER:** But it doesn't drown out the song. New Orleans. Nashville. Havana, sometimes.
> 
> **M.L.:** Havana? I've never heard anything from Havana! That's shortwave, isn't it?
> 
> **CALLER:** It is. I've listened a few times, but I don't speak Spanish.
> 
> **M.L.:** Mais, my English isn't great either, but there's good music!
> 
> **CALLER:** There is, isn't there?
> 
> **M.L.:** Oh, of course. Music, speech, sometimes I've even spoken to other operators, other amateurs. Standing in the cold, teeth chattering, listening to the crackle. \[*sighs*] Ah, it's great.
> 
> **CALLER:** A connection, yes? To become less lost.
> 
> **M.L.:** Oh, absolutely. Like I said, like I said – if we're all as lost as each other, it's other people who make us feel less lost, friend.
> 
> **CALLER:** We'd like to think that. How was the drive in, this evening?
> 
> **M.L.:** Oh, it's dreadful \[*laughs*] – it's getting so dark so early, hein? So I'm in my little Dauphine with its feeble little lights, just after dinner, trying to get up to the station without ending up in a ditch.
> 
> **CALLER:** Is it isolating, being out there in the dark?
> 
> **M.L.:** Oh, not really – when I step outside for a smoke, I can still see the city lights down the hill. That's who I'm talking to, isn't it, when I'm sitting here and speaking into the mic. And, of course, we have callers such as yourself. And it's not just me here – Giselle's in the control room, though she stepped out earlier.
> 
> **CALLER:** Well, you always have yourself to talk to.
> 
> **M.L.:** And callers like you! Don't sell yourself short, friend. If anyone's listening, I'm sure they appreciate our conversation, and, if they don't, we'll be back to the music soon enough \[*laughs*].
> 
> **CALLER:** Have you been following the hockey?
> 
> \[*c. 4 min: discussion of the Canadiens' prospects for the coming season. There is spirited disagreement about their chances.*]
> 
> **M.L.:** – and, well, we'll see, won't we? Béliveau's getting old, yes, but there's still fight in him.
> 
> **CALLER:** I suppose...
> 
> **M.L.:** You'll see. We'll see!
> 
> **CALLER:** We shall.
> 
> **M.L.:** Was there anything else on your mind, friend? Giselle's still out, taking a personal call or something, but she is so good, you see, I can still feel her phantom glare.
> 
> **CALLER:** You've had the dream too.
> 
> **M.L.:** I... pardon?
> 
> **CALLER:** We \[*inaudible*], and you \[*static*]. It's behind you.
> 
> \[*tape damaged – c. 12 s unrecoverable*]
> 
> **CALLER?:** Good to hear you resolved what's on your mind, friend. And thank you for the chat.
>
> **L.M.:** Bienvenue.
>
> **CALLER:** Have a nice morning. Next up, something relaxing from Louvain.
> 
> \[*CALLER hangs up*]
> 
> **L.M.:** Louvain, Louvain... ah! Here we are. Exactly where he left it.
>
> **03:25:** \[*crackle, as the record begins*]
>
> ## \[END LOG]

## Afterword
A copy of the log, heavily redacted for privacy, appears in the records of the *Commission d'enquête sur les phénomènes anormaux*, opened in 1958 in response to the Hudson Bay Disappearances. It was released in part in 1993 in response to a public records inquiry[^5].

While the programme log reports that there was no signal at the time, there were no complaints from listeners about the supposed gap in the programming. At least one copy of the conversation circulated on tape during the seventies, raising confusion about whether or not it was actually broadcast.

Laurent Michel \[sic] retired in 1991, and as far as can be determined, lived an entirely normal life. He married Giselle Tremblay in 1972.

[^1]: Ambiguity: "*on*" can mean "we", "one", "people" or "anybody".

[^2]: A later hand, writing in the margin, amends this to "*Cette Nouvelle Musique*". The note is undated and unsigned.

[^3]: From the telegraphic shorthand for distance. M.L. is referring to the practice of listening to distant AM radio stations when the signals bounce off the ionosphere. Atmospheric conditions made this easier at night.

[^4]: Another note in the margin – "M.L. is probably referring to LDEs \[Long-Delayed Echoes]. He thinks this is what it is talking about."

[^5]: This record is compiled from a (poorly) scanned and annotated transcription and translation published anonymously on the Internet Archive in 2019. The notes are in numerous different hands, and while it is dated to 1966, it is unclear in which order it was reviewed.
