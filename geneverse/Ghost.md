# Ghost

She made her way down the corridor, claws clacking on the linoleum. The painted concrete walls and informative posters about the latest Chiasm Syndicate initiatives coated the supernatural with sedimentary layers of boredom. But the press releases on McBrae reactor designs were *public,* they weren't what she was looking for.

The sign on the door said "executive offices". She needed to stand on her toes to peek through the window, revealing another long corridor, lined with doors, an utterly mundane-looking office, aside from the armed guards. Well, *those* were a sign that she'd read the map near the entrance right; that the 'Director's office' was actually this way.

The armed guard standing next to the door yawned, and she looked over at the young woman. She was standing watch over the corridor as if the intruder standing barely more than a metre away from her didn't exist at all. Which, well. *Did* she exist?

She stepped away from the door so the guard couldn't bump into her accidentally. "I want to open the door," she said, as clearly as she could around the jagged fangs that crowded her mouth. Her English was getting better, but her accent was still strong, not helped by the fact that, since she'd become a monster, she didn't have lips.

"I..." the guard said, her brow furrowing, as if she couldn't remember something.

She tried a different phrasing. "I need to open the door now."

The guard nodded to herself, turned to the door, punched in a code and started to pull it open.

Quickly, smoothly, she took a step forward. With one hand, she grabbed the door handle, and with her other hand, she reached up and covered the guard's eyes.

The guard made a small, confused noise, but there wasn't anything there. Her hands slackened on the door handle, dropping to her sides. The intruder waited for a few seconds. She didn't actually know *why* this worked – she had guesses, but who knew with a power like this, for all she knew, it made people forget they had eyes – but she let go of the woman's face and slipped through the door, letting it close behind her as the guard blinked, confused, before returning to her post. If she'd seen the small, purple-scaled creature that had just walked past her, her brain didn't let her register it.

The executive corridor had *carpet*, and she was grateful for the quiet as she padded along between the plain wooden office doors, glancing from nameplate to nameplate. Well. It wasn't like she needed to be truly stealthy. She could have screamed herself hoarse, grabbed people and shaken them, or even actually injured them, and they wouldn't even notice – she'd tried that, in the early days, after the change. It hadn't worked; people simply couldn't acknowledge her.

Dr A. Hemsley's door was identical to any other, unembellished, and it only gave her away because it said "director" beneath the name. The client had said in their letter that the good Doctor kept important, classified documents in her office, stuff that not even others in the company were allowed to see. Exactly *how* they knew that, she had no idea, but the client's letters – she insisted on letters because face-to-face didn't work, and her power even worked over the phone, somehow – were quite insistent, and they'd paid her fee upfront, so here she was. Certainly a change from bank robbery – this new "Benefactors' League" were starting to ask questions about tellers suddenly deciding to shove money into bags, then forgetting those bags existed after mysteriously losing them. She'd also found that she didn't really *need* money in the same way she always had – she could hardly buy food at a supermarket, and getting stuff from catalogues worked until the landlord of wherever she was staying demanded to meet in person. Her current arrangement was with a supervillain group, who had a better understanding of her... unique difficulties, and introduced her to a solicitor who handled questions about who owned a property. And, well. The Disharmonies could also introduce her to clients who could do with some espionage.

She reached out, turning the handle and gently pushing the door open. Beyond, the office wasn't huge – a desk, a bookshelf and a window overlooking the street. The desk was inhumanly neat, papers stacked square in an 'in' and 'out' tray, pens arranged across the top of the desk by size and colour, notebooks and other stationary lined up in neat regiments to a degree of precision that seemed... *concerning*. Behind the desk, Doctor A. Hemsley – *Alice* Hemsley, the nameplate on the desk supplied – sat, looking through a sheaf of slightly distressed and crumpled papers that were jarringly out-of-place in the rest of her meticulous office. A middle-aged woman with a sharp face, neat bun, and a light brown complexion, wearing a neat, navy-blue polo-necked sweater and tan overcoat.

She looked up as the door swung shut and her eyes – golden, piercing – looked straight through the intruder. She froze. Dr Hemsley was meeting her gaze. Could she... could she *see* her? For a moment, neither woman moved. Then, the moment passed, Hemsley adjusted her reading glasses and returned to her weird papers.

The intruder sighed with embarrassed relief. Of course, she couldn't see her. Silly, really, but old habits were old habits. She circled the room to look over Hemsley's shoulder, squinting at the papers she was holding – they looked like they'd been crumpled up, then straightened out and flattened again. She leaned closer to get a better look – what first seemed like thick green pen was actually *crayon*. Who wrote a report – an actual, *technical* report – in *crayon?* Reading a random page of it didn't really enlighten her, either – at least part of it was written in a language she didn't know or recognise the alphabet of, and the rest looked like annotated mathematics written over the top of a different report about a new type of turbine, doodled in the margins.

*Maybe the client would be interested,* she thought. She was basically leaning over Hemsley's shoulder at this point, not present in any sense except the physical, but still barely able to feel the woman's body heat through her clothes. Since the papers Hemsley was leafing through didn't seem to be getting any more interesting or making any more sense, she made a mental note to take another look at it when Hemsley put it down, and turned instead to one of the filing cabinets that sat behind her. Creaking it open, she flicked through the neatly-labelled dividers with her awkward talons; Abilities, Acquisitions, Admin... she skipped past a few folders... *Dimensional Research* looked interesting. Pulling a thin beige folder free, she laid it on the desk next to the impassive Hemsley and flipped it open. A wave of bizarre mathematics, hand-drawn diagrams – some of them in a familiarly green colour, a felt-tip this time – greeted her, of strange devices and plans for facilities that were larger than any buildings she'd heard of. Alongside the maths, the printed-out mugshot of a young man, glaring at the camera. The board he was holding said "HYWEL JONES", and someone had written "SCHISM" above his head. She *vaguely* recognised the name, but not the face – she'd seen reports of Schism on the news a few years ago; he'd been a supervillain who opened portals to 'other Earths'. Something about a mammoth rampage? She couldn't remember.

It seemed like something the client might want to see, though, so she retrieved her camera from her satchel, manoeuvring around the table until she was in the right light, and snapping a photo, as clear as she could. The camera buzzed as the photo slid out, and she gently took it and placed it on the table next to the document as she waited for it to develop. She'd need to take quite a few photos to get the gist of the documents as legible as possible. If this turned out to be what the client actually wanted, she'd have to come back and actually *steal* it later – the instructions were to let the Syndicate know as little as possible of her intrusion, so she was playing it safe. It took half an hour to take the photos and label them. Alice Hemsley had, meanwhile, pulled an apple, a small plate and a knife from a drawer in her desk, peeled the apple in one long, spiralling piece of skin, sliced it neatly up, and eaten it as she finished reading her weird crayon documents, before placing them in a neat folder and filing them under ‘Prognostication’, which was one of the oddly-named dividers that she’d noticed before. Hemsley sat back down, and the intruder took the opportunity to step over to the drawer and pull the folder out again. She took a few more pictures of the weird crayon scribblings. If the client wanted the Chiasm Syndicate’s secrets, these seemed suitably *strange*.

Satisfied that everything else in that filing cabinet was outside the scope of her instructions, she took a picture of the divider labels in the filing cabinets, in case the client decided later that they wanted to hire her for *mundane* industrial espionage in future, packed up, considered and rejected finding a photocopier – something that'd have to wait for a follow-up visit.  All the while, Dr Hemsley had been reading through some kind of internal briefing, making notes and fixing spelling mistakes with a blue pen.

She turned to leave, shouldering her bag, and stopped.

Dr Alice Hemsley was leaning against the door, continuing to read the briefing, pen in hand.

*When did she move?* She hadn’t heard her move. She’d just looked away for a second, and... *did she know she was here? How was that possible?*

She collected herself, then spoke. “I’m blocking the door. What if someone else came in?”

In response, Dr Hemsley reached down, locked the door, and returned to flipping through the papers she was holding.

The intruder stood there, stupefied, for a moment. *Was this on purpose?* She looked around the office desperately, for a distraction, for *something*. Inspiration struck, and she reached over the desk, picked up one of Hemsley’s neatly-arranged pens and placed it back down at an angle. Against the strict order of the desk, it stood out like a sore thumb.

“That pen’s in the wrong place,” she said, looking over at Hemsley. “I should fix that.”

Doctor Hemsley looked up, sighed, then strode over to the desk. The intruder darted towards the door, reaching out for the handle, claws fumbling with the lock. And then someone placed a hand on her shoulder and uttered four words that were, in context, the most terrifying ones she’d ever heard.

“Don’t leave just yet,” said Doctor Alice Hemsley. “I’d like to have a word.”
